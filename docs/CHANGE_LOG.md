## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Add the **grapefruit tree** and the fruit that hangs under it:
  - `aliment:grapefruit_log`, `aliment:grapefruit_leaves` and `aliment:grapefruit_sapling` are a small citrus of its own. It generates in the jungles and the savannas at one attempt per six chunks, and a sapling grows the same tree.
  - `aliment:grapefruit` is the fruit, and it hangs: vanilla's `minecraft:attached_to_leaves` decorator puts it in the block directly under a canopy leaf, and it can only exist while there is still a leaf or a log above it, so breaking the canopy drops it.
  - One grapefruit crafts into **eight** `aliment:grapefruit_slice`, which is the part a player eats: **2 hunger**, **3 saturation points**, **5 water**, **1 naringin** and **10 vitamin C** - the vitamin C being what a carrot is worth, because a slice is a plant food and a citrus besides. It is the only food in the mod that also drinks, and one of the few that can be eaten on a full stomach, because a player eats it for the naringin rather than for the hunger.
- Add the **grapefruit wood set**, a full second wood alongside the willow's: `grapefruit_wood`, `stripped_grapefruit_log`, `stripped_grapefruit_wood`, planks, stairs, slab, fence, fence gate, door, trapdoor, pressure plate, button, shelf, standing and hanging signs, a potted sapling, and its own **boat and boat with chest** - each with its own entity type, model layers and textures, as every wood's boat now needs. Its own `aliment:grapefruit_logs` tag is what its recipes take, so a plank recipe cannot be satisfied with willow logs, and it joins the vanilla `planks`, `logs`, `wooden_*`, `signs` and `fence_gates` tags, which is what makes the whole set axe-mineable and craftable the way any other wood is.
- Add **naringin** and the **CYP3A4** index:
  - Naringin is what grapefruit carries, 0..**10** - ten slices - and it is cleared over one game day. It has no effect of its own; everything it does, it does through the enzyme.
  - CYP3A4 runs 0..**100** and sits at **85** in a body that has eaten no grapefruit. Naringin is a step function on it rather than a curve, so the index only ever holds one of five values: **85** at 2 or below, **60** past 2, **45** past 4, **25** past 7, and **10** at 8.5 or above.
- Milk now counts as a drink, so drinking a bucket of it adds the standard **15 water**. In 26.3 vanilla's milk is a plain `Item` carrying a `CONSUMABLE` component rather than the `MilkBucketItem` of older versions, which is what lets the mod's `Item.finishUsingItem` hook see it at all.
- Add **grapefruit juice**: `minecraft:sugar` + one `aliment:grapefruit_slice` + a **water bottle** crafts one `aliment:grapefruit_juice`, which is the slice pressed into a bottle. It is a drink rather than a food, so it is **0 hunger** and always edible, and it takes the standard drink's **15 water** where the slice takes 5. It carries the slice's **1 naringin** and **10 vitamin C** unchanged, and adds **0.7 glucose** from the sugar - charged as bread is charged rather than as the plant food the fruit is, because sugar is the fast carbohydrate. Ten glasses is the naringin cap, exactly as ten slices is.
  - The ingredient is not `minecraft:potion` but a `fabric:components` filter requiring `minecraft:potion_contents` of `minecraft:water`, so the recipe will not accept an arbitrary brewed potion. Vanilla's `Ingredient` is a `HolderSet<Item>` and cannot express this, and a filter that fails to parse drops the recipe silently, so the self test both asks the recipe manager for the recipe by name and asserts that a water bottle matches while an awkward potion does not.
  - Its sprite is drawn in **vanilla's own potion style**, the convention the mod's other potions already follow: vanilla's `potion.png` bottle and `potion_overlay.png` liquid, with the greyscale liquid tinted to the slice's flesh so the glass reads as grapefruit. The bottle is vanilla's pixel for pixel; only the tint is ours.
- Add the **grape vine** and **grape wine**, a second drink out of the fermentation tank:
  - `aliment:grape_vine` is a four-stage crop that follows the vanilla rule for what a plant may sit on: **dirt, grass, coarse dirt, podzol *and farmland*** - it is placed against `minecraft:supports_vegetation`, the tag that already includes `minecraft:farmland`, so a seed goes onto tilled soil exactly as it does onto grass. It grows through its stages on bone meal or on random ticks. Only `age=3` carries fruit: it drops **1-3** `aliment:grape`, and every earlier stage drops nothing, so picking one green wastes the seed. It grows wild across the plains and the temperate forests, which is the only way to get the first bunch, because the seeds come from the fruit.
  - A ripe vine is harvested by **right click**, not by being broken: the pick drops **1-3** grapes and knocks the vine back to **`age=1`**, one stage short of ripe, so the plant survives and fruits again. This is the shape of vanilla's sweet berry bush and of the five Aliment plants that already work this way (phellodendron, ephedra, coptis, licorice, seaweed). Before `age=3` a right click yields nothing and is **passed through** rather than consumed, so bone meal and seed placement still reach the block. Breaking a ripe vine still pays the same 1-3 grapes - there is no item for the vine itself - so the two harvests differ only in whether the plant is left standing.
  - Farmland under a vine **stays farmland**: `aliment:grape_vine` is named in `minecraft:maintains_farmland` alongside wheat and the pumpkin stems, so a planted vine is one of the crops that keeps vanilla from reverting the block to dirt behind it. Without that entry the vine would grow on soil that crumbled under it.
  - `aliment:grape` is the fruit: **2 hunger**, **1.0 saturation**, always edible, and `aliment:grape_seeds` comes two per grape in a crafting grid. A seed is sown straight into the ground and grows the vine back.
  - `aliment:grape_wine` is a **5%** drink. It is a **separate item** from `aliment:wine` rather than a change to it: the existing 7% wine and its 40% distillation are untouched, and a player reaching for a glass of grape wine is choosing the weaker drink on purpose.
  - The fermentation tank makes it from **grapes + sugar + water + brewer's yeast**. Grapes and sugar go in — in either order — and turn the liquid purple; the sugar *completes* the must rather than replacing the grapes, and **yeast is refused until both are in**, because a grape is mostly water and the sugar is what actually ferments. 45 seconds later the tank holds grape wine at 5%, which bottles straight out with no still - unlike beer, whose mash must be distilled.
  - The three tank recipes are now distinguishable by what is in the water: **sugar** gives 7% wine, **wheat** gives beer, and **grapes + sugar** gives 5% grape wine.
- Add **optional Farmer's Delight integration**. The recipe half is data-only: no `fabric.mod.json` entry, and no reference to any Farmer's Delight class. Every integration file is gated on `"fabric:all_mods_loaded"` for `farmersdelight`:
  - A **knife** on a cutting board cuts one `aliment:grape` into three `aliment:grape_seeds`, and one `aliment:grapefruit` into eight slices.
  - The **cooking pot** boils a single grapefruit slice with sugar into `aliment:grapefruit_juice`, drawn into a **glass bottle** you supply - the same `container` field its own soups use to ask for a bowl, and the reason the pot will not give you a glass without one.
  - Aliment also publishes conventional `c:` tags of its own - `c:crops/grape`, `c:seeds` and `c:drinks` - so any mod that reads the conventional tags finds the grape, the seeds and the wine. These load whether or not Farmer's Delight is present, and they add to that mod's tags rather than replacing them.
- Add **physiological values for every edible Farmer's Delight food** - all **80** of them. The recipes above were data-only, but what a food *does to the body* cannot be JSON, because it has to run inside the ingestion hook; so this half is the mod's first code-level integration with Farmer's Delight, and it is built so that the mod still boots and plays identically without that mod installed (see below).
  - **Sodium** comes only from the **cured pork**: bacon, cooked bacon, ham, smoked ham (**+0.8** each), the bacon sandwich and bacon and eggs (**+1.2**), and the honey-glazed ham (**+2.0**). Deliberately below the 3.0 of a salt serving, because a rasher is salty without being a spoonful of salt - and nothing else has sodium added, because Farmer's Delight adds no salt item and **no recipe of its own calls for salt**.
  - **Vitamin C** goes to the plants, on the scale the mod already used (an apple is 12, a carrot 10, a melon slice 8): a tomato **+10**, a cabbage **+8**, a tomato sauce **+8**, an onion **+6**, a pumpkin slice **+6**, a cabbage leaf **+4** - and to the dishes in proportion to what went into them, so a gleaming salad is **+18**, a fruit salad **+16**, a ratatouille **+16**, but a sandwich that merely garnishes with a slice of tomato is **+4**. Meat, eggs, bread, rice, milk and chocolate carry none: the vitamin is in the plants, so a grilled salmon has no vitamin C however it is cooked.
  - **Iodine** goes only to the two kelp rolls, the only foods made with `dried_kelp`: a roll is **+0.30** - three dried kelp (0.20 each) wrapped around a bowl of rice, charged as a dish containing kelp rather than as three dried kelp, because the rice is most of it - and a slice, which the cutting board yields three of, is a **+0.10** third of that.
  - **Glucose** is charged by the tiers the model already had, with one new band: an assembled plate - a stew, a sandwich, a pasta - is **+0.6**, between plant food (0.4) and bread (0.7), because the meat and fat in it slow the starch down. Charging every cooked meal as bread would make it a sugar spike. So the mod's cooked rice and honey cookies take the bread figure, its dishes the new mixed-dish figure, its cuts **+0.5** cooked and **+0.4** raw, and its vegetables and salads **+0.4**.
  - **Drinks and risk.** Its bottled milk, three sweetened drinks (apple cider, melon juice, hot cocoa) and nine soups count towards the water index, exactly as vanilla's milk bucket and mushroom stew do; the three sweetened ones also take the sweet-drink glucose. Its raw dough, raw pasta, chicken cuts and nether salad - the four it tags `c:foods/food_poisoning` - roll for a bacterial infection, as does every raw cut, because a Farmer's Delight cut is raw meat.
  - **Still fully playable without it.** The dependency is `compileOnly`: it puts the mod on the compiler's classpath and **not** in the shipped jar, so the release neither bundles Farmer's Delight nor demands it, and `fabric.mod.json` does not name it. Because the classes are then absent at runtime, the integration is layered across four files so that the guard the rest of the mod touches names nothing of that mod at all: only the item table and the recipe helper name its types, and the item table does so solely in its static initialiser, which the JVM runs only when the object is first used. Nothing reaches it unless `isModLoaded("farmersdelight")` is true, so the mod never loads a Farmer's Delight class and cannot throw `NoClassDefFoundError`. Verified by running the whole suite with the dependency removed from the dev run: **243 passed, 0 failed, no error**.
  - Carrying the dependency has a second benefit: naming all eighty items as `ModItems` fields rather than registry-id strings means the **compiler** checks every one, so a Farmer's Delight update that renames an item fails the build instead of silently dropping a food from the model.

### Changed
- **Berberine is now cleared by CYP3A4**, where before it was cleared at a fixed rate. The rate is scaled by the enzyme index against the 85 baseline, so at 85 the metabolism is exactly the one the model always had and at 10 the same dose takes eight and a half times as long. It is the first interaction in the mod where one thing a player eats changes how long another lasts: a single coptis herb clears in about 9,400 ticks on its own and about 17,800 with a body full of grapefruit.
- **`aliment:salt_water` and `aliment:crude_salt_water` now take a real water bottle**, not any potion. Both named a bare `minecraft:potion`, so until now a potion of healing or of fire resistance could be stirred with salt and come back as salt water, quietly destroying the potion. They now use the same `fabric:components` water filter the grapefruit juice does. This is a **breaking change for anything that fed those recipes a non-water potion**, which is the intent.

### Fixed
- **The fermentation tank can no longer be topped up with water once it has fermented.** The tank stores ethanol as a *concentration* rather than as a total, so a water bucket poured into a tank that still held wine refilled it to three levels and the alcohol came back out at full strength: bottle one, refill to three, bottle three more, and one dose of sugar and yeast yielded without limit. Both the water bucket and the water bottle now refuse a tank whose ethanol is above zero. The water the tank held when fermentation finished is the whole batch; bottling it empty clears the ethanol and the tank is fillable again. Filling an idle tank is unaffected, which is what the plain-sugar and wheat paths still rely on. The self-test drives that exploit end to end - bottle, refill, bottle again - and asserts the batch still yields exactly one bottle per water level; with the guard removed the same loop produces 8 bottles from 3 levels.
- **The brine cauldron can no longer be replaced by a water cauldron.** It was the only Aliment cauldron that did not override `useItemOn`, so it inherited `AbstractCauldronBlock`'s, and that dispatcher is vanilla's `EMPTY` one - which registers the empty-bucket water interaction. A water bucket therefore **replaced the whole block with a full water cauldron**, silently destroying however far the evaporation had got, and a lava bucket did the same. Brine now returns `PASS` for every item, which is the correct answer because no item is meant to be used on it: the concentration is carried by the `stage` property and not by a fill level, so there is nothing for extra water to dilute. The stirring rod is unaffected - `AlimentInteractions` handles it before vanilla is consulted. The other three cauldrons already overrode the method and never reach the dispatcher.
- The **potted willow sapling** now renders as a potted willow. Its blockstate pointed at `minecraft:block/potted_oak_sapling`, so it had always drawn itself as a potted *oak* while the correct `aliment:block/potted_willow_sapling` model sat unused beside it. Found while adding the grapefruit's, which had been mirrored from the same file.
