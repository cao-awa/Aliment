# Changelog

All notable changes to the **Aliment** mod will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/),
and this project adheres to [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

---

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Grapes and grape juice now carry vitamin C of their own: 4 each.** Both were plant foods already and collected the **+5** supplement, but neither had a figure of its own, so a bunch of grapes was worth exactly the 5 and a glass of juice exactly the 5 - the mod credited the fruit with no ascorbate at all. They are worth **4 + 5 = 9** now.
  - The figure goes on the **grape**, and the juice takes the grape's own, which is the rule the grapefruit already follows: a glass is the fruit pressed into a bottle, so it can be worth neither more nor less than what went into it. Adding it to the juice alone - the shape the original grape-juice entry described - would have made the drink worth more than the fruit it is pressed from, which is the wrong way round for a fruit whose whole point is that it is eaten as well as drunk.
  - **4** rather than a carrot's 10: a grape is a soft fruit eaten by the handful, not a root vegetable, so it lands under the berries' 6. Small is the honest figure and it is deliberately not zero - a vineyard is now a real answer to scurvy, which it could not be while the fruit was worth the bare supplement.
  - Two assertions pin it and they are written against the **total** rather than as `own + supplement`, because the latter is satisfied by any value of the constant including the zero this change exists to remove. Beside them sit the two bounds that make the size of the figure a claim rather than a number: a grape must come out **above** a potato, which has no figure of its own, and **below** a carrot.
- **Crushed rock salt: the ore is now mined into a shard, and the shard is what the grindstone turns into crude salt.** Rock salt ore no longer drops itself. It drops **one crushed rock salt**, and that shard is the grindstone's input for the **nine crude salt** the ore used to give up directly, so the chain gains a step at its head - `ore -> shard -> crude salt x9` - without the yield at the end of it moving a single item. A stack of ore still becomes the same nine crude salt per ore it always did.
  - The ore keeps its **item form**, so it is still placeable, still in the creative tab and still grindable in one step: grinding the *block item* yields one shard, exactly as grinding what it drops does. That is the shortcut rather than the rule - the shard is the haul, and the block-to-shard row exists so an ore already in a player's inventory is not stranded between two recipes.
  - The sprite is a new one, drawn by a new `tools/gen_rock_salt_shard_texture.ps1` in the **pink halite palette** the ore block's own crystals use - so a chunk this colour is visibly the same mineral as the ore it came out of, and is unmistakably not the beige `crude_salt.png` heap it grinds into. The two sit side by side in the grindstone input and the creative tab, which is what makes that distinction worth drawing rather than reusing a heap.
  - It is drawn in the **style of vanilla's raw ore items**, which is the reference a lump of dug-up mineral wants to be read against: one chunky off-centre mass, a nine-step tonal ramp, a shaded rim that is lighter where it faces the light and darkest where it turns away, and a jagged lower edge. The ore block's five crystal tones are all in the ramp with the midpoint between each adjacent pair filled in, so the shard sits in the ore's exact colours rather than merely near them.
  - The shading is **dithered**, and that was the one part of this that had to be learned from the reference rather than assumed. The first version of the grid was a clean diagonal ramp, which at 16x16 renders as a smoothly lit sphere instead of rock; raw iron's rows alternate tone constantly. Each interior pixel is now its diagonal position nudged one step by a fixed hash of its own coordinates, so the body still darkens across its width but the surface is irregular - and because the hash is fixed the scatter is identical on every run, which keeps the generator idempotent and the grid in the script still an exact description of the file.
  - It is a shard rather than a powder deliberately: the whole point of the new step is that mining breaks rock and the grindstone makes salt, and an ore that dropped *powder* would have collapsed two different actions into one word. **Just Crude Salt** still fires on the grindstone, now worded for the shard, and its description changed in all four languages with it.
- **Grape juice: the drink the fermentation tank makes, before the yeast.** One item, two ways to make it. At a crafting table it is a **grape, sugar and a water bottle**; in a Farmer's Delight **cooking pot** it is a **grape and sugar**, drawn out into an **empty glass bottle**. It is the unfermented half of what the tank does with the same two ingredients, and the reason to make one is the reason it exists at all: it carries **no ethanol whatever**, so it is what a player drinks when they want the grape and not the drink. A full glass of water, the sugar's **0.7** glucose, zero hunger and always edible - a glass of juice is not a meal, and a full stomach should not stand between a player and a drink they made on purpose.
  - It takes the **grape's own vitamin C**, which is now **4** - and the grapes are worth the same 4, so the two forms of the fruit agree and pressing one neither invents ascorbate nor throws it away. (This bullet first read "the grape's own vitamin C, which is to say none": grapes used to carry no figure of their own and the glass fell back to the bare plant supplement. Both the fruit and the juice have a figure now, and adding it to the grapes rather than only to the juice is what keeps the pair honest - a drink cannot be worth more than the fruit it is pressed from.) The sugar is what makes it a sweet drink rather than a plant food at the glucose tier, and the glass still collects the **plant supplement** below at +5, so it lands on **9**.
  - Its sprite is drawn by the existing `tools/gen_grape_textures.ps1` in the **vanilla potion style** every other drink here uses: vanilla's own `potion.png` bottle and `potion_overlay.png` liquid, with the liquid tinted to the purple of the ripe grapes. The bottle mask is identical to the grape wine's to the pixel, so the pair reads as one drink at two stages - which is what they are.
  - The pot recipe is gated on `fabric:load_conditions`, so it simply does not exist without Farmer's Delight. The compat layer answers `false` for everything without the mod rather than failing.
- **A fever and the ache that comes with it are now on the HUD.** Everything the temperature did to a player used to be invisible: the weakness, the mining fatigue and the shimmer at the edges of the screen all arrived on their own, and the only way to find out how ill you were was to ask the diagnostic command. Two indicators say it instead, in the same row as the vanilla effects. **`aliment:fever`** has three grades, appearing at **38.5**, **39.5** and **40.0** °C, and **`aliment:pain`** is a single level from **39.5** °C.
  - Neither of them does anything. They carry no attribute modifier, they do not tick, and nothing in the mod reads them back - the weakness and the mining fatigue are exactly what they were. The self test asserts that, and it also asserts that the fever grade reads *higher* than the symptom tier it is judged against, so the two scales cannot quietly collapse into one.
  - The grades are a second scale rather than a re-cut of the symptom one. `thermalTier` still has exactly two fever steps, and the new middle grade at 39.5 changes nothing about what is done to the player - it is there because 39.5 is worth hearing about even though it is no worse than 38.6 was. The test pins the grade on both sides of all three thresholds, and separately requires the symptom tier to be identical either side of 39.5; if that ever stops holding, the grade has become a symptom and the middle step is what has to come back out.
  - They sit on the bar for exactly as long as the condition holds, with **no countdown**. A duration would put a timer next to the icon, and a timer answers the wrong question - what ends a fever is the body cooling down, not six seconds elapsing - so they are added with an infinite duration and taken off by hand the moment the temperature drops back under its threshold. That also closes a trap: vanilla keeps the *stronger* of two amplifiers when an effect is reapplied, so an implementation that only ever added would leave the icon reading **Fever III** after the fever had fallen back to **Fever I**.
  - Both are ordinary effect sprites found by the effect's own registry id, so they are drawn by a new generator, `tools/gen_effect_textures.ps1`, and named in all four languages.

### Changed
- **Plant food and both kinds of meat raise blood glucose by half of what they did.** The three plain-food figures go from **0.4 / 0.4 / 0.5** to **0.2 / 0.2 / 0.25** (plant food / raw meat / cooked meat). A normal meal of bread and vegetables was walking a body into the insulin response rather than anything the player had done wrong, and it charged a carrot exactly what it charged a steak.
  - **Bread is deliberately untouched at 0.7**, and so are sweetened drinks at 0.7 and assembled dishes at 0.6. Refined starch really is the fast carbohydrate, and "worse than what grows in a field" is the whole point of that band; the two others are judged by what was *done* to the food rather than by what it grew as. The halving is one-sided on purpose, and the test now pins it that way - it asserts each new value against the old one divided by two *and* that bread still reads 0.7, so a later edit cannot quietly "tidy" bread down with them.
  - One consequence worth naming: **the glucose figure no longer identifies the kind of food.** Plant food and raw meat are both worth 0.2 now, so anything that needs to tell them apart must ask the classification instead of comparing the number. The water rule below is exactly such a place, and the tests cover the trap directly - a classifier written as "adds the plant figure" would have hydrated a raw steak.
- **Plant food now carries the water that was in it: +0.4 a serving.** Fruit, vegetables, kelp, seaweed, mushrooms and every willow bark soup hydrate *slightly*, which is the honest answer for food that is mostly water by weight; a player living on them was previously dehydrating while eating. It is a fortieth of what a drink is worth, so it is nowhere near a substitute for one, and it is deliberately not enough to notice as a mechanic - what it removes is an absurdity, not a difficulty.
  - Nothing that already carries water is stacked with it. Every drink has just been given its glass, and the **grapefruit slice** - the one food that is also a drink, with a third of a bottle of its own - keeps its own **5**. That is three separate exclusions, and each is asserted by eating the item for real and reading the body, because the failure this guards against is not a big number in the wrong place but the wrong *food*.
  - Farmer's Delight is on the same rule: its vegetables, salads and fruit carry the plant water, while its soups are drinks and its assembled plates are meals, and neither is charged it. The compat layer answers `false` without the mod, so nothing changes for a player who does not have it.

- **The CYP3A4 index now slides down instead of snapping.** Naringin was a *step function* on the enzyme, so the index only ever held one of five values and the whole inhibition arrived the instant a threshold was crossed. It is now a **curve**: the activity is exactly the calibrated value at the naringin it was calibrated for - **85** in a body that has eaten nothing, **60** at two slices, **45** at four, **25** at seven and **10** at eight and a half - and it interpolates between them, so one more slice always takes a little more off the liver rather than everything changing at once. Every calibrated value and every one of its naringin knots is unchanged; what is gone is the flat plateau between them. Each segment is a smoothstep, `3t^2 - 2t^3`, whose value and slope are both zero at either end, so the curve is flat *at* each knot and has no kink where two segments meet either. Past eight and a half slices it holds, which is still what makes that the deepest the inhibition goes.
  - The self test asserts the property rather than the table, because a table of five values cannot tell a curve from a staircase: it sweeps the whole naringin range in steps of a hundredth of a slice and requires the largest move the index makes to stay inside the steepest segment's own slope. The step function fails that outright, moving **25 points** in a single step. Alongside it, the curve is pinned to its six calibration points - including the flat run from eight and a half to the cap - and swept for any rise and any escape from the reported range.
- **Grapefruit consequently holds berberine down for longer.** The plateaus the index used to sit on are gone, so the liver runs lower through the middle of a dose: a single coptis herb still clears in about **9,400 ticks** on its own, but after nine slices of grapefruit it now takes about **20,100** rather than the 17,800 the step function gave - a little over twice as long instead of a little under. The shape of the interaction is unchanged, and so is every number it was calibrated from; only the descent between them is now a slope.
- **Peaceful difficulty now freezes the whole system, and the sugar-only hold is gone.** Peaceful used to hold *only* the blood glucose, on the reasoning that the sugar curve was the one thing in this mod that took hitpoints away by itself. That reasoning was too narrow: a potassium excess, the sepsis of a severe infection and its tissue damage all take hitpoints off with no difficulty setting to intercept them either. So the partial mechanism is deleted and peaceful is switched off through the same door creative mode already used - `AlimentSymptoms.isFrozen` is now `player.isCreative || player.level().difficulty == Difficulty.PEACEFUL`, one switch instead of two that could disagree about whether a body was being simulated at all.
  - Everything the hold did, it still does, because the model is simply not advanced: nothing moves on a peaceful body, food and injections included. What is new is everything *else* that used to keep running. No effect is applied, no damage is dealt, no mining penalty is charged and no exhaustion multiplier is applied.
  - What the mod has **already** put on the player comes back off, and three of those would otherwise never have come off at all. The two HUD indicators are added with an infinite duration and removed by hand, so a body frozen at a fever would have worn the icon indefinitely; the mining penalty is an **attribute modifier** with no lifetime of its own, so a player frozen mid-penalty would have kept the slower pickaxe forever. Both are cleared on the frozen path now. The ordinary vanilla effects are deliberately left to lapse instead of being cleared, because clearing them by hand would mean guessing which of them the mod was responsible for and would take the player's own potion of weakness with it.
  - The plumbing is gone rather than left unused. `tick` and `addGlucose` no longer take a hold flag on either side of the seam - the Scala model, `AlimentModelBridge`, and the Kotlin facade - so there is no way left to tick a body partially, and nothing is computed and then discarded. Every call site came out with it: the command's `statusLines` parameter and the `[held: peaceful]` tag it printed, the two ingestion and symptom checks, and the self tests built around them.
  - `/aliment status` therefore prints nothing about being frozen, which is a decision rather than an omission: the readout describes the body as stored, and a frozen body is one nothing is happening to rather than one whose numbers mean something different. A tag for it would have to go on every line to be honest, which is a readout explaining its own absence instead of the body.
  - The one consequence worth stating plainly is unchanged from the hold: a player who enters peaceful *already* crashing stays crashing, because the cause and the cure go off together. Nothing in the mod asks for damage on peaceful any more - it cannot, because the model is not advanced - so what keeps that from being lethal is `ServerPlayer.tickRegeneration`, which heals **1.0 HP a second** whenever health is below maximum. Disable `naturalRegeneration` and that arithmetic reverses, which is vanilla's business rather than this mod's.
  - The self test now checks the freeze from both sides, because every assertion in it is a negative one - nothing moved, nothing was applied, nothing left on the screen - and a body that was healthy to begin with would satisfy all of them while proving nothing. So the fixture's liveness is pinned separately (one tick really does move each axis, and a meal really does raise the sugar), and the freeze is released again at the end to prove the model resumes.

- **An electrolyte is now cleared faster the more of it there is.** The leak was one flat number per mineral, so a body carrying far too much sodium lost it at exactly the rate a healthy body lost its own - the excess could only be washed out by drinking, and nothing about the concentration itself mattered. The loss is now scaled by the value: the filtered load a kidney handles is what is in the blood, so `loss * excretion * (value / normal)` replaces `loss * excretion`. At normal the factor is exactly **1**, so the steady state and every existing calibration are untouched; what changes is that a high electrolyte leaves faster than a low one, and the higher it is the faster it leaves.
  - The per-mineral multipliers are unchanged, so the order they leave in is preserved - chloride and sodium first, magnesium and calcium last - and only the *rate* became concentration-dependent. The self test asserts the proportionality itself rather than a table of values: it puts two identical bodies at different concentrations, ticks both, and requires the higher one to have lost more in absolute terms.
- **Contact with an animal no longer all but guarantees an infection: 5% a second is now 1%.** The proximity roll is still against every living `Mob`, still arms its one-second cooldown, and still delivers the same +5 viral load when it comes up - but a passive mob standing against the player for a full minute now infects about **45%** of the time rather than almost certainly, and a body that only brushes past a cow usually walks away with nothing. Farming is a risk a player chooses to take rather than a tax on keeping animals.
- **Blood glucose now costs a third of what it did, and a surplus is shed faster than ever.** At the top of the reference range the drain drops from the original 1.5 mmol/L over two days to the same 1.5 over **six**, so a body that never eats lasts three times as long between meals - the fasting floor is still 3.5, it simply takes six game days to reach it instead of two, and the hypoglycaemic crisis that used to arrive on day eight now arrives around day **eighteen**.
  - **Above 5.5 the rate goes the other way.** The excess is cleared at **1.5x the original** rate - explicitly *not* 1.5x the reduced one - so a spike comes down 4.5 times faster than the normal band and is pulled back under 5.5 more briskly than it was before this change. Slowing the ordinary fall must not make an overshoot linger, or the two halves would work against each other; there is a deliberate **step** at 5.5 rather than a ramp, and the test pins both rates and the ratio between them rather than the curve.
  - The original two-day rate is kept in the model as a named yardstick rather than deleted, because both new rates are stated against it - the normal band as a third of it, the excess as half again of it - and writing either one as a bare fraction would hide which of the two it was derived from.
- **The camera shake is half as strong, and the immune system's share of it now waits for a cytokine storm.** Every amplitude coefficient is halved - base, per symptom tier, storm, calcium and thermal alike - while the frequency and the chance of a shake are untouched, so a body in trouble still shakes exactly as *often*; it is simply no longer being yanked about. The amplitudes moved out of Kotlin and into the model while this was done, which is where every other magnitude lives anyway.
  - The immune term was a **x2 multiplier** on the symptom chance during a cytokine storm, which meant a body with nothing visibly wrong could shake hard the moment inflammation crossed the storm line, and one just under it could not shake at all. It is now an **addition gated on the storm threshold of 75**, and it is calibrated so that a storm adds exactly what the doubling used to - a symptomatic body at the storm still shakes **0.4** - so the frequency in the worst case is unchanged. What changed is that nothing below a storm shakes from inflammation at all, however far above an ordinary response it has climbed.
  - The gate sits on the storm threshold rather than on an onset of its own, and it is a **step rather than a ramp**. It was first written as a slope from an onset of 40, which left a band where a body that was visibly well on its way to a storm already moved the camera; tying it to the storm is also what lines the *chance* of a shake up with its *amplitude*, since the storm's larger amplitude has always begun at exactly 75. A mild infection is now completely silent on the camera.
- **Every plant food now carries a vitamin C supplement of +5.** It is an addition, never a replacement, so an apple's own 12 becomes 17 and a carrot's 10 becomes 15; a plant with no figure of its own - a potato, an onion - is worth exactly the 5. The point is that a diet of fruit and vegetables stops being marginal: it was possible to eat vegetables all day and still run at a deficit, which made the deficiency a bookkeeping problem rather than a survival one. The juices count, too, so a glass of grapefruit or grape juice is worth what the fruit in it was, and the rule that stops drinks being stacked with the plant **water** is untouched - the supplement and the water are separate questions.
- **Iodine deficiency now needs to be deeper before it shows.** The reference floor is **0.25**, down from 0.40, so a player gets *no* symptom at all until iodine has fallen halfway from normal to the hard floor. The thyroid is a late responder, and a deficiency that produced symptoms at 0.39 was saying a great deal about a body that had barely lost anything. Everything the thyroid drives reads the one number, so the set point, the deficit symptom and the status readout all move together, and severe hypothyroidism still waits for its own threshold at 0.20.
- **Iodine is weaker across the board, and takes much longer to run out.** Every dietary source is **halved** - kelp 0.05, dried kelp and raw seaweed 0.10, cooked seaweed 0.125, seaweed iodized salt 0.20, a Farmer's Delight kelp roll 0.15 and a slice 0.05 - and the store now drains from 0.50 to the 0.05 floor in **15 game days** rather than 3, a leak of **0.03** a day. That reverses the maintenance diet: **one kelp a day is now comfortably enough** where two used to be needed and one was not, so the choice is no longer a standing chore. (The drain was first moved to 5 days and 0.09 in this same release; it is the sixth of a day's kelp that settled it, because a rule that makes a single missed helping harmless should not also make the pantry the centre of the game.)
  - A surplus is now cleared **three times** faster than the leak, in proportion to how far above normal it is, so an excess drains away sooner than the flat rate would manage **without bending the depletion curve**: the extra term is written against the excess above normal rather than against the whole value, which is what keeps "fifteen days from 0.50 to the floor" an exact figure. The two changes pull in opposite directions on purpose - a body that is only just keeping up holds its iodine far longer, while a body carrying a pile of it gets rid of the pile quickly. The self test pins both halves - the flat fifteen days from normal, and a surplus losing more than a normal store over the same ticks - because one number cannot hold both claims. It also pins the new diet directly: one kelp a day now *beats* a day's leak, which the old arithmetic had the wrong way round.

### Fixed
- **`/aliment status` now really does print every metric.** It is documented as *every physiological metric, in one readout*, and it had drifted a long way from that: the whole **glucose chain** - the glucose itself, the body's own insulin and injected insulin aspart - was missing from it, and so was the **liver**, meaning both the naringin load and the CYP3A4 index the grapefruit interaction turns on. All four are quantities the model runs on and the player had no way to see. They are on the readout now, the glucose with its reference-range tag and the liver pair printed directly under the berberine it clears, so that "the coptis is lasting because of the grapefruit" reads in one place instead of two.
  - Three more things were missing for the same reason: the two new **indicator** effects with their grades, and the **immune-response latch** - the flag that says the body has noticed an infection. That one trips well before the load is high enough to make the player feel ill, and it is the reason inflammation can be climbing during a stretch where nothing appears to be wrong.
  - The **condition line** had the same hole from the other side: a body in a hypoglycaemic crash was announced as `healthy`, because the ladder never asked about glucose. It does now, and the crash gets its own rung ahead of the electrolyte imbalances - it is the only state on that ladder that takes hitpoints off by itself.
  - The line building is split out of the command handler as `statusLines(data)`, for the same reason the glucose meter's message is: so the self test can assert the text the command really produces rather than one it built for itself. The assertion that earns its place is **completeness** - the test reads the fields `AlimentData` actually declares, pairs each one with a fragment of text only its own line can produce, and requires every one of those fragments to be present in the output. A field added to the state without a line here now fails the test by name instead of quietly going unprinted, which is precisely how the glucose chain went missing in the first place. (The first version of that check compared the field list against a second list of field names, which no edit to the readout could ever have failed - deleting the liver line left it green. It is the fragment, matched against the returned text, that makes it a claim about the readout.)
  - Note that `/aliment set` still cannot touch glucose, insulin or naringin; the readout is complete before the setter is.
